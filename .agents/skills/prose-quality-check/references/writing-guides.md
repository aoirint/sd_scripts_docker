# Writing Guide Rationale

These English technical and web-writing guides inform the structure review.
Their numerical suggestions are contextual editorial heuristics, not universal
limits or a basis for Japanese character caps. Apply semantic unity, audience,
genre, navigation, and preservation of meaning before counts.

## Sections

[Microsoft's heading guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/scannable-content/headings)
recommends dividing substantial content into meaningful subtopics without
manufacturing subdivisions or filler. It sets no paragraph-count ceiling.

## Paragraphs

[Google's paragraph guidance](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/paragraphs)
centers a paragraph on a coherent unit of reasoning. Its sentence-count advice
also warns against excessive one-sentence paragraphs. Use counts to prompt
review of focus and reader effort rather than automatic splitting.

## Sentences

[Google's short-sentence guidance](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/short-sentences)
recommends one idea, removing unnecessary words, and clarifying complicated
embedded enumerations. It does not impose a universal word-count ceiling.
Related conditions and consequences still need an intelligible connection.

## Lists

[Microsoft's list guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/scannable-content/lists)
favors related, parallel, scannable items. Its suggested item count is qualified
by "if possible," and it permits occasional longer items.
[Google's list guidance](https://developers.google.com/style/lists) also allows
multi-paragraph items. Neither supports an unconditional one-sentence rule.

One independently reviewable point per item is this Skill's working default,
not a quoted universal rule. Closely related elements and necessary supporting
details can stay together. List length warrants regrouping only when meaningful
categories or easier scanning justify it; preserve complete requirements and order.
